Draft guide: from your first pick to a complete deck

Start with strong, flexible cards. As the draft progresses, shift your attention to the colors, curve and roles your deck needs.

Early picks: keep your options open

A powerful rare, useful removal spell or strong single-color card can give you a starting point. If two cards look close, consider which one leaves more room to change direction. Your first pick does not have to determine your final colors.

Choosing colors: look for a pattern

Watch which colors keep offering playable cards. A useful card appearing late can be a clue that a color is less contested, but one card is not enough to be sure. Color requirements and narrow synergies can also explain why a card is still available.

Later picks: fill the gaps

As your deck takes shape, check your early plays, removal, mana sources and ways to win. Another expensive threat may do less for your deck than a card that helps you reach the turns when those threats matter.

Using card rankings

Compare the set, archetype and sample size behind an evaluation. A card's overall rating is a starting point; the cards you have already drafted may change its value to your deck.
